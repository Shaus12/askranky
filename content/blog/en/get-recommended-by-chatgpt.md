# How to Get Your Local Business Recommended by ChatGPT (2026 Guide)

**Short answer:** You can't pay ChatGPT to recommend you, but you can make it far easier for it to do so. ChatGPT names the local businesses it can find, understand and verify: clear information on your own site, a consistent footprint across listings and reviews, and mentions in the "best of" pages and directories it reads. Fix those, then track the same questions weekly.

## Why this matters for local businesses
Customers increasingly type full questions into ChatGPT and Gemini, things like *"Who's a good emergency plumber in Austin who works weekends?"*, instead of scanning ten blue links. The answer usually names only a handful of businesses. If you aren't one of them, that customer never sees you, even if you rank well on Google.

There's no "Search Console for ChatGPT", so most owners simply don't know whether they're being mentioned.

## How ChatGPT picks local businesses
OpenAI doesn't publish a formula, but when you look at the answers and the sources they cite, the same patterns keep showing up:
- **Live web search.** When ChatGPT searches, it relies on a search index. Bing is widely reported as a major source for ChatGPT search, so make sure Bing can find you.
- **Third-party lists and directories.** Local "best X in City" articles, review sites and directories are cited constantly, often more than the businesses' own sites.
- **Reviews and your Google/Bing profiles.** Volume, recency and specificity all help.
- **Clarity.** If your site doesn't plainly say what you do, where and for whom, the model can't match you to the question.

## The 10-step checklist
1. **Run a baseline.** Write 5–10 real customer questions. Ask each in a fresh chat in ChatGPT and Gemini. Log who's named, in what order, and which URLs are cited.
2. **Write a 2-sentence identity statement:** "[Business] is a [type] in [city/area] specializing in [2–3 services] for [customers]. [One verifiable differentiator]." Use it everywhere.
3. **Put it at the top of your homepage, as real text.** Don't put it in an image or a slider. List services, service areas, hours and your phone number right below.
4. **Make NAP consistent.** Use the same name, address, phone and description on Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook, Apple Business Connect and your industry directories.
5. **Claim Bing Places and verify Bing Webmaster Tools.** It's free, and it's often skipped.
6. **Add an FAQ page that answers like you do on the phone.** Cover price ranges, timing, areas served and "X vs Y" choices, with 2–4 sentences each.
7. **Add LocalBusiness schema** (or a specific type such as `Dentist`, `Plumber` or `Attorney`) and FAQPage markup that matches what's visible on the page.
8. **Ask for specific reviews.** "Fixed our water heater in South Austin same day" helps more than "Great service!" Never buy reviews.
9. **Get into the sources AI already cites.** From your baseline, list the 3–5 most-cited URLs for your category and city. Request a listing, pitch an update, or offer a quote or data point.
10. **Don't block AI crawlers.** Check that robots.txt (and any Cloudflare "block AI bots" setting) allows OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User and GPTBot (plus the crawlers of any other AI tool you care about), and that key content is in the HTML, not loaded only by JavaScript.

## Copy templates
**Review request:**
> "Hi [Name], thanks for choosing us for [service]! If you have a minute, a short Google review would really help, especially if you mention what we did and where: [link]."

**Pitch to a "best of" list:**
> "Hi [Editor], I'm [Name] from [Business] in [City]. I noticed your guide to [topic]. If you update it, we'd love to be considered: [one verifiable fact: years in business, specialty, hours, certifications]. Happy to send anything you need."

## What doesn't work
- **Asking ChatGPT to "remember" your business.** Your chat doesn't change what other users get.
- **Buying placement.** Where ChatGPT shows ads, they are labeled and shown separately from the answer, and OpenAI says they don't influence it.
- **Keyword stuffing or "best in the USA" claims.** The models look for specific, verifiable facts.
- **Judging by one answer.** Responses vary by wording, location, conversation history and model version.

## How to measure progress
Track the same fixed questions weekly, on each engine:
- **Mention rate:** the share of answers that name you
- **Position:** first, third, or not at all
- **Competitors:** who gets named instead
- **Cited sources:** which sites the engine leans on, and whether yours is among them
- **Trend:** a four-week average beats any single screenshot

## FAQ
**How long does it take?**
There's no guaranteed timeline. On-site changes can show up once engines re-crawl, while third-party mentions usually take longer. That's why you track weekly.

**Is this different from SEO?**
It builds on SEO. The emphasis shifts from ranking a page to being named and cited in an answer, which is why off-site mentions and clear, direct answers matter more. This is often called GEO (generative engine optimization).

**Does the same work help with Google AI Overviews and Gemini?**
Largely, yes. Your Google Business Profile, reviews, clear answers and structured data help across engines.

**Will what I see in ChatGPT match what my customers see?**
Not exactly. Answers vary by person, location and session. Measure trends across repeated runs.
